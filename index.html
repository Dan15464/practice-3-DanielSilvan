Piet Mondrian

Pieter Cornelis Mondriaan, known after 1911 as Piet Mondrian, was a Dutch painter and art theoretician, who is regarded as one of the greatest artists of the 20th century.

Composition with Red Yellow Black Grey and Blue ", painted in 1921, stands as a testament to Mondrian's neoplastic philosophy. At first glance, the painting presents a grid of intersecting horizontal and vertical lines that form a series of squares and rectangles.

1872 - Born in Amersfoort, the Netherlands

1911 - Moves to Paris and sees Cubism

1917 - Co-founds De Stijl with Theo van Doesburg

1930 - Paints Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

1944 - Dies in New York

Less, until it holds

Mondrian started as a landscape painter, Trees, windmills and dunes, painted in the ordinary way. In Paris he saw what Picasso and Braque were doing and began breaking his subjects into planes and lines. Within a few years the subject were gone and only the lines remained.

He called the result Neoplasticism, and he set rules for it: only horizontal and vertical lines, only the three primary colors plus black, white and grey, and no symmetry. The rules were not a limit. They were the point. With everything else removed, the relationships between the parts had to carry the whole picture.

The 1930 Composition is one of the clearest examples. One red square most of the canvas. A small blue block and a smaller yellow one sit at the bottom corners. The black lines are not all the same width, and the white areas are not all the same white. It looks simple from across a room and turns out not to be.

The lines

only horizontal and vertical. Mondrian painted them by hand, and up close the edges are not prefectly stright. They stop short of the canvas edge in places, which keeps the grid from feeling like a cage.
